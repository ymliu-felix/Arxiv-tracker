When Evidence Vanishes: Distilling Visual-Evidence Dependency in Medical Vision-Language Models
Authors: Qizhen Lan, Yiling Ma, Ruiqi Chen, Mengchen Fan, Xi Xiao, Lin Zhao, Liang He, Baocheng Geng, Pulin Che, Tianyang Wang, Xiaoqian Jiang
First: 2026-10-16T13:48:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T13:48:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Compact medical vision-language models (VLMs) can answer clinical questions correctly without reliably using the image, relying instead on question wording or learned priors. Standard knowledge distillation copies a teacher's answer preference on the original image, but it leaves unchecked whether that preference is conditional on the visual evidence required by the question. We study this missing target as medical visual evidence dependency: support for the correct answer should decrease when the matched evidence is absent. We introduce Evidence-Conditioned Dependency Distillation (ECDD), which pairs each training image with a control matched to the evidence level and distills the teacher's drop in answer margin across the pair. A lower bound objective transfers positive dependency by penalizing only shortfalls, while original-image supervision preserves task accuracy. The same formulation applies when the evidence is a queried clinical region, a surgical instrument, or whole-image visual content. Across three medical VQA settings, ECDD keeps original-image accuracy competitive with distillation and alignment baselines while making answer support more sensitive to evidence removal. Under the DME headline objective, for example, the positive-case margin response to target rather than random masking rises from 0.786 with KD to 2.023 with ECDD, while original-image accuracy differs by 0.11 points. These results suggest that medical VLM distillation should transfer both the teacher's answer preference and its dependence on visual evidence.

HydraLLM: Adaptive Width Slicing for Elastic Text and Vision-Language Models

Abstract
LLMs and LVLMs are increasingly deployed across heterogeneous hardware, ranging from data-center GPUs to resource-constrained edge devices. Their fixed-size architectures limit the ability to reuse a single model across deployment settings with different compute, latency, and model-size targets. We introduce HydraLLM, an elastic MQA-based architecture. Its number of active attention heads determines the effective widths of the embeddings, attention projections, feed-forward layers, and the output head, forming a nested family of subnetworks within a single parameter set. We introduce a static-to-elastic conversion pipeline that, starting from a pre-trained model checkpoint, includes importance-driven weight reordering and stochastic subnetwork training with knowledge distillation. We extend this principle to multimodal inference through an elastic projection module whose output width follows the active text width. HydraLLM covers a wider range of active parameter configurations than FFN-only elastic architectures. With 75% of heads active, HydraLLM achieves 2.2 percentage points higher TriviaQA accuracy than a state-of-the-art nested baseline configuration which has 17.25% more parameters. With 50% of heads active, our multimodal model achieves a CLIPScore comparable to that of the 7$\times$ larger Gemma 3 4B model. With only 25% of heads active, HydraLLM increases token-generation throughput by 75.3% over the full-width configuration.

SETAREH Transform: A Sorting-Assisted Haar-Wavelet Sparsification Method for Approximate Inner-Product Computation
Authors: Seyed Kamaledin Setarehdan
First: 2026-10-16T11:42:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T11:42:29+00:00
Comments: 22 pages, 2 figures, 5 Tables, 21 References
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern deep learning systems increasingly rely on high-dimensional vector operations, in which large input and parameter vectors are repeatedly stored, transferred, and processed. Although substantial research has focused on reducing neural-network model size through pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and efficient architectures, the storage, memory-traffic, and communication costs associated with repeatedly presented high-dimensional input vectors remain important practical considerations, particularly in distributed and edge-computing environments. This paper proposes the SETAREH (Sorting-assisted Energy-concentrating Transform for Approximate Representation using Haar wavelets) transform, a Haar-wavelet-based sparsification approach for approximating the inner product between a fixed high-dimensional coefficient vector and successive input vectors. By applying the same sequence of permutations and Haar transforms to both vectors, SETAREH preserves the exact inner product before truncation. It then retains only a subset of the transformed coefficient-input pairs, reducing their representation cost while introducing a controllable approximation error. To evaluate SETAREH, we consider two complementary experimental settings: a synthetic Gaussian-vector baseline and a real apparent-age estimation task using a convolutional neural network on the APPA-REAL dataset. The results show that SETAREH can approximate the evaluated inner products using substantially fewer transformed coefficient-input pairs than the original vector dimension. The findings support its use as a potential structured data-representation method for repeated inner products with fixed weights, while the practical benefit depends on approximation accuracy and the cost of storing or transmitting permutation metadata.

Replay Pruning and Few-Bit Integer Inference for Hardware-Efficient Speed-Sign Recognition
Authors: Se Un Park, Kyungkeon Chung, Huigeol Kim, Junyoung Park
First: 2026-10-16T01:41:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T01:41:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Speed-limit sign recognition on an automotive camera system-on-chip (SoC) must fit a detector and a classifier (the reader) into the memory, arithmetic, and energy budgets of the device. In our two-stage recognizer, the detector, a YOLO-family network with ReLU and hard-sigmoid operators, is compressed by channel replay: an iterative pruning cascade identifies the channels to remove, the recorded deletions are replayed on the original task-trained network, the parent, and one continuous knowledge-distillation recovery follows. On a YOLOv5n, replay removes 60.3% of the parameters at the accuracy of the parent and, in validation mAP50-95 over five matched seeds, outperforms iterative pruning and training from scratch, and its per-step variant (per-step replay) is the most accurate pruning method. On a YOLOv8n, replay and per-step replay outperform every other method and the unpruned parent. The reader, a five-layer CNN with 18k parameters, and the post-processing of the YOLOv5n and YOLOv8n heads use integer arithmetic only. With 4-bit weights and 8-bit activations (W4A8), the reader matches FP32 accuracy in 12.1 KB of on-chip memory, and an executable streaming model reproduces the INT8 class predictions of its runtime on all 9,850 test crops. On a Jetson AGX Orin, INT8 is faster than FP16 only in the implicit formulation of TensorRT, and the replay detector runs fastest on the deep-learning accelerator (DLA). Trained on public data only, the combined system of 0.72 M parameters recognizes 95.2% of the signs of a held-out automotive camera at 90% precision and 94 frames per second.

Clean Data Can Still Carry Backdoors: Support-Persistent Backdoors for Model Reuse
Authors: Junhoo Lee, Baekseung Kim, Seungyeon Kim, Nojun Kwak
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-15T04:04:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T04:04:19+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Traditional backdoor attacks inject artificial trigger patterns into training data, causing models to misclassify triggered inputs while maintaining normal behavior on clean samples. Since these artificial triggers are absent in clean datasets, standard knowledge distillation (KD) typically eliminates them, leading to the common belief that KD serves as an effective purification defense. In this paper, we propose DAR (Discover-and-Relabel), a support-persistent backdoor method that retains its behavior even after clean-data distillation. DAR identifies naturally abundant patterns in low-dimensional feature subspaces using subspace clustering and applies label-only poisoning to samples that inherently satisfy the discovered rule. At test time, the backdoor is activated by locally modifying the corresponding subspace of the target images. By instantiating spatial and frequency operators, DAR achieves attack accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art backdoor attacks in ImageNet classification and CLIP-based prompt tuning. Notably, DAR keeps attack success above 96% after clean KD and remains defense-resistant in practice. Project page: https://junhoo.me/backdoor

Does Latent Chain-of-Thought Actually Reason? A Single, Unifying Taxonomy for Latent Reasoning
Authors: Jingjie Qiu, Sheldon Yu, Xunyi Jiang, Jiajin Liu, Zihan Huang, Zhendong Chu, Tong Yu, Julian McAuley, Junda Wu
First: 2026-10-15T03:37:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T03:37:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Latent chain-of-thought replaces written reasoning steps with continuous hidden states, giving models additional steps to compute before answering. It remains unclear what computation these steps actually perform. High task accuracy and decodable intermediate answers do not establish that a model uses its latent chain to reach an answer. We study this question across twenty-one model-task configurations covering three training recipes, three reasoning tasks, and two backbones. We combine chain deletion and content replacement with probes and trajectory analysis to test whether the chain affects the answer, what it encodes, and how its states change. On logical tasks, COCONUT often carries an answer that is already readable before the chain begins. Its states settle early, and deleting the chain leaves predictions almost unchanged. Per-step supervision produces an ordered layout of reasoning steps and can make the model depend on the chain. Yet even an ordered chain can act as a placeholder, with deletion hurting performance while content replacement leaves answers unchanged. On arithmetic tasks, all tested recipes show dependence on chain content. These results show why readable latent states alone are insufficient evidence of reasoning. We introduce a taxonomy that separates what a chain represents from how the model uses it. Seen this way, earlier reports no longer compete: placeholder tokens, collapsed superposition and step-dependent causal roles are each consistent with a different kind of chain, each obtained on a different task, training recipe or measurement. Once a study says which property it tested, whether the model uses the chain, what the chain holds, or how its state moves, claims about latent reasoning can be compared.

HERO: Preserving Structure and Semantics in Heterogeneous Continual Graph Learning
Authors: Guiquan Sun, Xikun Zhang, Jingchao Ni, Dongjin Song
First: 2025-05-23T04:37:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:48:39+00:00
Comments: under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Machine learning on heterogeneous graphs has advanced rapidly, driven by the diverse entities, relations, and semantics inherent in real-world data. However, most existing studies assume static graphs, whereas real-world graph data are often continuously updated. Continual learning in this setting is particularly challenging because historical knowledge is encoded not only in target-node representations, but also in their heterogeneous structural contexts and relation-dependent semantic responses. To this end, we introduce HERO, a heterogeneous continual graph learning framework that preserves historical knowledge at both structural and semantic levels while adapting to incoming tasks. We analyze experience replay as an approximation to the unavailable historical gradient and decompose its error into target-selection and context-reconstruction terms. This analysis shows that replaying target nodes alone is generally insufficient for relation-sensitive HGNNs, as removing typed neighborhood context can alter both representations and historical gradients. Motivated by this, HERO employs DiSCo to select representative target nodes and reconstruct compact heterogeneous contexts through relation-aware multi-type neighbor expansion. To further preserve knowledge that cannot be captured by replayed subgraphs alone, HERO aligns task-conditioned predictions and heterogeneous semantic responses through multi-level knowledge distillation. A lightweight look-ahead adaptation step is additionally used to improve plasticity on incoming tasks. Experiments on four datasets with three HGNN backbones show that HERO achieves the best or tied-best average performance among state-of-the-art baselines, while maintaining competitive forgetting. Our code is available at https://github.com/gqBond/HCGL.

Abstract
Graph Collaborative Filtering models have achieved remarkable success in recommendation systems. However, they inherently suffer from a catastrophic representation collapse in strict cold-start scenarios due to the topological isolation of unobserved items. While integrating multi-modal semantic features offers a potential remedy, directly mapping isolated semantic attributes into a globally topology-driven collaborative space frequently precipitates representation degradation and optimization conflicts. To bridge this fundamental structural divide, we propose DualGCN, an innovative Dual-Branch Graph Knowledge Distillation Framework. Operating under a decoupled two-stage training paradigm, DualGCN first constructs a robust collaborative teacher network where item representations are anchored by a topology-smoothed semantic prior, effectively reshaping the latent manifold. Subsequently, a semantic student branch employs a residual mapping mechanism and a magnitude-agnostic cosine distillation objective to achieve high-fidelity cross-modal alignment without disrupting established collaborative signals. Furthermore, we introduce a dynamic norm reconstruction mechanism to rigorously calibrate the physical scales of novel and warm items during global retrieval. Extensive experiments across three real-world benchmark datasets demonstrate that DualGCN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in strict cold-start scenarios while comprehensively preserving recommendation accuracy for highly active items.

Platonic Projection Structures: Operator-Induced Observability in Representation Learning
Authors: Kazuo Ishii, Bishnu Prasad Gautam, Jieling Wu, Saher Javaid
Venue: Entropy 2026, 28(7), 768

Comments: 29 pages, 7 figures. Published in Entropy
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We characterize observability in representation learning through Platonic Projection Structures (PPS), an operator-theoretic framework for analyzing representation accessibility under partial observation. Rather than treating observable outputs as direct reflections of latent representations, PPS models observation through a self-adjoint positive semidefinite operator acting on a latent representation space. A system is represented as a triple $(H, Π, O)$, where $H$ is a latent representation space, $Π\succeq 0$ is an observation operator, and $O(v)=\langle v,Πv\rangle$ defines an induced scalar observable. Observability is characterized by the quotient geometry $H/\ker(Π)$, representing equivalence classes of latent states indistinguishable under observation. We show that quantum measurement and representation inference under linear observation models share this operator-theoretic structure while differing in the algebraic properties of their observation operators; the correspondence is structural rather than physical. Representation transfer and knowledge distillation can likewise be interpreted as approximate preservation of observable geometry through $ΦΠ_T \approx Π_S Φ$. PPS also reveals a structural limitation of output-based interpretability: latent components in $\ker(Π)$ are inaccessible from induced observables, imposing intrinsic constraints on attribution and explanation methods. Controlled empirical validations demonstrate kernel-invariant observability, projection-induced attribution gaps, and rank-controlled observable geometry in latent representation spaces. PPS thus provides an explicit characterization of observability through operator-induced quotient geometry and a unified perspective on representation accessibility, interpretability, and projection-mediated inference.

Authors: Yiping Li, Fan Zhao, Zhibo Hou, Zhiyu An, Wan Du

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) is increasingly studied as a cheaper alternative to reinforcement learning, yet it remains a form of knowledge distillation (KD) that relies on teacher supervision. We revisit OPD from the KD perspective by asking where the teacher's advantage lies along student-generated trajectories. Controlled token flips at teacher-student disagreements directly measure the accuracy gained from teacher corrections. Single-token flips yield a modest average gain on selected failed trajectories, whereas repeated flips raise accuracy from 28.4% to 74.5%, close to the teacher's 76.9%. Gains continue to accumulate with more flips, but mass- and KL-based selection outperform random flips by 15-18 percentage points at counts of similar magnitude. Selective intervention remains useful after OPD, but greater argmax agreement does not consistently improve accuracy across model pairs. We advocate training schedules that learn more valuable teacher corrections while maintaining distributional stability. Controlled flips and fork-resolution analysis evaluate OPD beyond accuracy by measuring which teacher preferences the student acquires and what further correction can recover.

Multi-View Relational Distillation for Spatial Reasoning with Vision-Language Models
Authors: Kiet T. Nguyen, Hanbo Shim, Jinwoo Kim, Seunghoon Hong
First: 2026-08-11T12:34:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T11:26:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) have achieved strong image and video understanding, yet their visual-spatial representations remain geometrically fragile, leading to failures in spatial reasoning needed for embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous driving. Prior approaches to geometry grounding either fine-tune VLMs on spatial question answering, which can perpetuate spurious visual representations, or fuse features from large geometry-grounded vision models, which substantially increases model size at inference. Knowledge distillation from geometry-grounded vision models offers an alternative, but directly matching multi-view teacher features can disrupt the pretrained alignment between visual and textual representations, degrading object- and language-semantic capabilities. We propose multi-view relational distillation (MVRD), which distills patch-wise cosine similarities across views instead of the teacher features themselves. These relations encode geometric correspondences adequate for spatial understanding, while leaving the student representation underdetermined, allowing it to remain close to its pretrained vision- language space. Across representative VLMs, MVRD improves visual-spatial reasoning, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and feature distillation while approaching feature fusion methods with considerably fewer added parameters and lower latency. We show that MVRD makes visual representations more geometric while retaining language alignment, and generalizes to 3D scene understanding tasks such as object grounding, dense captioning, and question answering.

Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have achieved strong performance on diverse vision-language tasks, but their computational demands limit deployment under constrained budgets, particularly on edge devices. Many works focus on Knowledge Distillation (KD) and try to transfer knowledge from powerful teacher models to students. In addition to reducing model size, visual token compression provides a complementary route to efficiency, but introduces mismatched visual token counts that complicate direct token-wise knowledge transfer. To mitigate this, we propose DVTR-KD, a knowledge distillation framework based on \textbf{D}ense \textbf{V}isual \textbf{T}oken \textbf{R}econstruction, which reconstructs the teacher's dense visual tokens from the student's compressed tokens. We reconstruct visual states after the final LLM layer and at the input to an intermediate layer identified as important for visual-information readout. At the intermediate layer, reconstruction is supervised through the teacher's response predictions rather than by direct token-wise KL or MSE matching. An auxiliary attention-readout loss complements this response-level supervision by aligning how the student and the original teacher integrate contextual information when predicting responses. Evaluations on diverse benchmarks demonstrate that DVTR-KD improves the performance of token-compressed students while retaining the inference efficiency afforded by visual token compression.

Efficient Multi-Granularity Knowledge Transfer for Radiology Report Generation

Abstract
Radiology report generation can automatically generate clinical descriptions from X-ray images, thereby significantly improving the efficiency of radiologists. This task is challenging because it requires medical knowledge to accurately identify diseases and describe them in a professional manner. However, existing methods often overlook the importance of enhancing medical knowledge in describing pivotal areas, a capability that requires models to effectively extract and aggregate knowledge at multiple levels of granularity. Accordingly, we herein propose a novel and compact Efficient Multi-Granularity Knowledge Transfer (\textbf{EMGKT}) method to address the above issues. First, we encode global knowledge embeddings using a medical vision-language model, which provides contextual medical knowledge. Moreover, we devise a novel Fine-Grained Knowledge Distillation (FGKD) training task which efficiently extract fine-grained knowledge. Specifically, the FGKD training task contains teacher embeddings and student embeddings. Teacher embeddings are encoded using extra priors; while student embeddings are learned from the teacher embeddings through knowledge distillation. During inference, the student embeddings are used to enhance fine-grained knowledge while the teacher embeddings are discarded, resulting in negligible computational costs and no need for extra priors. Finally, we further develop a mixture of disease diagnosis expert classifiers to enhance knowledge extraction. The classifiers are initialized using disease embeddings and are modeled as different experts to address various granularity features. Notably, \textbf{EMGKT} can be efficiently applied to most existing methods. Extensive experiments are conducted on two widely-used public datasets and various baselines, which demonstrates the effectiveness and transferability of \textbf{EMGKT}.

Abstract
High-capacity neural network models have achieved state-of-the-art performance across diverse classification tasks, yet they frequently operate as black-box models, lacking the transparency necessary for critical decision-making. This paper proposes a solution to address this gap: the NeuroRule knowledge distillation framework that results in explainable rule-sets from neural network models. Representational Difference Distillation
Authors: Abdulkarim Mugisha, Divin Irakiza
First: 2026-10-11T23:57:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-11T23:57:03+00:00
Comments: 9 pages, 7 figures, 1 table
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Knowledge distillation transfers representational knowledge from a large teacher network to a compact student. Existing relational and contrastive distillation methods treat all samples uniformly, spending equal gradient on well-matched and poorly-matched regions of the representation space alike. We propose Representational Difference Distillation (RDD), which diagnoses where teacher and student representations structurally disagree and concentrates the distillation signal on those regions. RDD uses two asymmetric affinity matrices that capture opposing directions of misalignment: one identifies samples the student incorrectly considers similar, the other identifies teacher relationships the student has yet to learn. These affinities determine which sample pairs the distillation loss aligns and how strongly it corrects each misaligned sample. On CIFAR-100 with resnet32x4 to resnet8x4, RDD reaches 75.58% test accuracy in our runs, on par with CRD (75.45%) and above KD and RKD. We additionally explore curriculum-based and sampling-based variants, finding that weighting the loss is more effective than filtering the data.

Abstract
