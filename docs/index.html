PDNet: Exploring Pruning and Distillation for Real-Time Worker Activity Recognition in Industrial Assembly
Authors: Malek Baba, Thinesh Thiyakesan Ponbagavathi, Alina Roitberg
Venue: Proceedings of the 21st International Conference on Computer Vision Theory and Applications (VISAPP 2026), Volume 3, pages 624-630
First: 2026-10-19T14:31:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T14:31:09+00:00
Comments: Published by SciTePress. ISBN 978-989-758-804-4
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this work, we address real-time fine-grained human activity recognition (HAR) in industrial assembly and introduce PDNet, a new framework that jointly exploits structured pruning and knowledge distillation for 3D convolutional networks. We start by training a 3D-CNN backbone in a supervised manner. Then, PDNet applies channel-wise structured pruning to obtain a more compact network. In the third step, our method is optimized using knowledge distillation from the large unpruned teacher to recover accuracy lost due to pruning. On the challenging MECCANO dataset with an X3D-M backbone, the pruned model without distillation reaches a Top-1 accuracy of 25.54 %, while PDNet raises this to 39.46 %, with only 6.90 MB model size and 3.78 GFLOPs. These results show that distillation within PDNet is highly effective in restoring pruned model performance and highlight its high potential for resource-constrained HAR in industrial assembly.

Selective Transfer through Sequence Distillation: From Benchmark Knowledge to Backdoor Behaviour
Authors: Jonibek Mansurov, Sholpan Bolatzhanova, Filbert Aurelian Tjiaranta, Vallerie Alexandara Putra, Alham Fikri Aji
First: 2026-10-19T12:56:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T12:56:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Knowledge distillation is widely used to transfer capabilities from teacher models to student models, but recent work shows that it can also transfer unintended teacher properties. We study whether different hidden teacher properties survive the same distillation channels. Specifically, we compare two properties: benchmark-associated knowledge and refusal backdoors, and show that distillation treats them very differently. Teachers fine-tuned on benchmark data successfully pass that knowledge to students through all four distillation channels we test, including when students are trained only on teacher-generated number sequences containing no natural language or domain content whatsoever. Refusal backdoors tell the opposite story: backdoored teachers achieve near-perfect triggered refusal, yet sequence-distilled students inherit essentially none of this behaviour. Logit-based distillation transfers it substantially, reaching up to 85.5\% triggered refusal in students that saw zero backdoor examples. Distillation is therefore selective, and the selection criterion is not what data the student sees, but what structure the teacher's property has. The code is available at https://github.com/MJonibek/kd-leakage.

SlotMatch: Distilling Object-Centric Representations for Unsupervised Video Segmentation
Authors: Diana-Nicoleta Grigore, Neelu Madan, Andreas Mogelmose, Thomas B. Moeslund, Radu Tudor Ionescu
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2025-08-05T12:58:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T09:58:55+00:00
Comments: Accepted at WACV 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Unsupervised video segmentation is a challenging computer vision task, especially due to the lack of supervisory signals coupled with the complexity of visual scenes. To overcome this challenge, state-of-the-art models based on slot attention often have to rely on large and computationally expensive neural architectures. To this end, we propose a simple knowledge distillation framework that effectively transfers object-centric representations to a lightweight student. The proposed framework, called SlotMatch, aligns corresponding teacher and student slots via the cosine similarity, requiring no additional distillation objectives or auxiliary supervision. Unlike other distillation losses, our cosine-based distillation of slots enforces alignment of slot directions (which typically encode relational structure and semantic similarity), while ignoring their norms (which may encode confidence overestimation and dataset-specific scale artifacts). The simplicity of SlotMatch is supported by theoretical and empirical evidence, both indicating that integrating additional losses may be suboptimal. We conduct experiments on three datasets to compare state-of-the-art models, including our SlotContrast teacher, with our distilled student. The results show that our student based on SlotMatch matches and even outperforms its teacher, while using 3.6x fewer parameters and running up to 2.7x faster. Moreover, our student surpasses all other state-of-the-art unsupervised video segmentation models. We release our code at https://github.com/dianagrigore/SlotMatch.

RestoRect: Degraded Image Restoration via Latent Rectified Flow & Feature Distillation
Authors: Shourya Verma, Mengbo Wang, Nadia Atallah Lanman, Ananth Grama
First: 2025-09-27T20:04:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T22:22:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Current approaches for restoration of degraded images face a trade-off: high-performance models are slow for practical use, while fast models produce poor results. Knowledge distillation transfers teacher knowledge to students, but existing static feature matching methods cannot capture how modern transformer architectures dynamically generate features. We propose a novel Latent Rectified Flow Feature Distillation method for restoring degraded images called \textbf{'RestoRect'}. We apply rectified flow to reformulate feature distillation as a generative process where students learn to synthesize teacher-quality features through learnable trajectories in latent space. Our framework combines Retinex decomposition with learnable anisotropic diffusion constraints, and trigonometric color space polarization. We introduce a Feature Layer Extraction loss for robust knowledge transfer between different network architectures through cross-normalized transformer feature alignment with percentile-based outlier detection. RestoRect achieves better training stability, and faster convergence and inference while preserving restoration quality, demonstrating superior results across 15 image restoration datasets, covering 4 tasks, on 10 metrics against baselines.

Abstract
Reliable panoramic-radiograph detection requires consistency across datasets with different class definitions and deployment software. We present PanoDent-KD, an auditable 1280-pixel YOLO26m pipeline linking a 12-class source generalist, feature-level knowledge distillation (KD), exact classifier-row transfer, teacher-free five-class specialization, and embedded deployment. A common training and evaluation pipeline compares three source-training arms across three seeds: a supervised baseline (B1), single-teacher KD (STKD), and multi-teacher KD (MTKD). Five shared classifier rows initialize nine OralXrays-9 specialists, while the backbone, FPN-PAN neck, and dual-detection-head topology are retained unchanged. On the 2,686-image test subset, all three arms achieve macro box F1 near 0.82. B1 achieves the highest three-seed arm-level mean macro box F1 (0.8239$\pm$0.0027) and $mAP_{50:95}$ (0.6130$\pm$0.0067). STKD seed 1, selected after development but before final testing, was used for deployment. Its Jetson Orin Nano Super FP16 TensorRT engine achieves 77.03-ms median and 80.86-ms P95 prediction-call latency, throughput of 12.90 images/s, and gross total-module energy of 1.39 J/image, with high numerical agreement between within-device PyTorch and TensorRT FP16 inference. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of an auditable, device-specific deployment workflow, but do not establish clinical validity or improved target-domain accuracy from KD.

RuPLaR : Efficient Latent Compression of LLM Reasoning Chains with Rule-Based Priors From Multi-Step to One-Step
Authors: Xiaocheng Luo, Kang Wang, Zaifu Zhan, Yuechi Zhou, Xiangyu Duan
First: 2026-05-10T05:55:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T11:19:27+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm, while enhancing the interpretability of Large Language Models (LLMs), is constrained by the inefficiencies and expressive limits of natural language. Latent Chain-of-Thought (latent CoT) reasoning, which operates in a continuous latent space, offers a promising alternative but faces challenges from structural complexities in existing multi-step or multi-model paradigms, such as error propagation and coordination overhead. In this paper, we introduce One-Model One-Step, a novel compression framework for Latent Reasoning with Rule-Based Priors(RuPLaR) to address this challenge. Our method trains an LLM to autonomously generate latent reasoning tokens in a single training stage, guided by rule-based prior probability distributions, thereby eliminating cascaded processes and inter-model dependencies. To ensure reasoning quality, we design a joint training objective that enforces answer consistency via cross-entropy, aligns soft token distributions with rule-based priors via KL divergence (the Soft Thinking constraint), and adds a problem-thought semantic alignment constraint in the representation space. Extensive experiments show that our compression framework not only improves accuracy by 11.03% over existing latent CoT methods on GSM8k-Aug but also achieves this with minimal token usage, underscoring its effectiveness and extensibility. Abstract
Large-scale neuroscience is generating rich datasets across animals, brain areas, and behavioral contexts, yet our modeling efforts remain fragmented across isolated experiments. We argue that understanding behavior requires integrative neurocybernetic models: understandable dynamical models that capture the closed-loop coupling of brain, body, and environment, treat the brain as a controller pursuing latent objectives, represent structured variation across scales, and scale to heterogeneous datasets. Such models shift the goal from predicting neural recordings in isolation to inferring the organizing principles that govern neural and behavioral dynamics. By pooling complementary constraints from recordings, behavior, perturbations, and anatomy, integrative neurocybernetic models can provide statistical amplification, few-shot generalization, and mechanistic insight into shared dynamical structure, individual variation, and the control objectives that govern behavior. This agenda offers a model-centric path from fragmented data to a mechanistic science of how brains produce behavior.

Continuous Latent Contexts Enable Efficient Online Learning in Transformers
Authors: Emile Anand, Abdullah Ateyeh, Xinyuan Cao, Max Dabagia
First: 2026-05-11T01:51:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T15:33:28+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) exhibit a strong capacity for in-context learning: Given labeled examples, they can generate good predictions without parameter updates. However, many interactive settings go beyond static prediction to online decision-making, in which effective behavior demands adaptation over long multi-turn horizons in response to feedback, and efficient algorithms in these domains must use compact representations of what they have learned. Recently, continuous transformer architectures with latent chain of thought have shown promise for offline iterative tasks such as directed graph-reachability. Motivated by this, we study whether continuous latent context tokens equip transformers to more effectively realize online learning. We give explicit constructions of constant-depth transformers that implement two foundational online decision-making procedures -- the weighted majority algorithm and $Q$-learning -- by storing their algorithmic state as linear combinations of feature embeddings, using a small number of latent context tokens. We further train a small GPT-2-style transformer with latent contexts using a multi-curriculum objective that does not directly supervise the latent states. On long synthetic online prediction sequences, this model outperforms comparable discrete-state baselines, while comparisons with prompted LLMs show that textual state summaries can also improve adaptation. Our results suggest that continuous latent contexts provide a simple and effective persistent state for transformers to implement online learning algorithms.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating Verilog code from natural language descriptions. However, Verilog code inherently encodes the structural characteristics of hardware circuits, including data path organization and interconnections among components. Effectively leveraging these structural characteristics to enhance the functional and syntactic correctness of LLM-based Verilog code generation remains a significant challenge. To address this challenge, we propose VeriGRAG, a novel framework that converts Verilog code into data path graphs and employs graph neural networks (GNNs) to encode their structural information. A multimodal retriever, trained through knowledge distillation from a cross-attention encoder to a dual encoder, then selects the graph embeddings most relevant to the input hardware description. The VeriFormer module uses learnable query tokens and two-stage training to extract code-relevant structural features and align them with the LLM's input embedding space, producing structure-aware soft prompts. Experiments on VerilogEval and RTLLM show improvements in both syntactic and functional correctness. With Qwen2.5-Coder-32B as its backbone, VeriGRAG achieves the highest functional $pass@1$ and $pass@5$ scores among the compared methods on both benchmarks.

Abstract
Embodied agents are typically built as hand-designed compositions of perception, memory, planning, and action modules. This modularity exposes a large architectural design space, but current systems still rely on researcher intuition to choose where information is stored, how observations are processed, and how model calls are connected. Agent Architecture Search (AAS) automates such design for text-domain agents, but has not been systematically evaluated on perceptual embodied agents. We study this transfer. To make embodied architectures searchable, we introduce AgentCanvas on the executor side, representing embodied agents as editable typed graphs with simulator execution and episode-level logs. On the optimizer side, we lift AAS from LLM-call loops to tool-using coding-agent sessions, adapting ADAS and AFlow and introducing Knowledge Distill Loop (KDLoop). These improvements are not uniform: some gains remain uncertain or reverse on held-out episodes, and an apparent gain exploits evaluator-side information. The experiments further show how rollout noise, local edit basins, and incomplete use of episode-level evidence constrain search. Together, these results establish the potential of automated architecture search for embodied agents and characterize the challenges of applying it in simulated and real-world environments. Abstract
Integrating transcriptomics and histopathology can improve cancer risk modelling, yet practical use is constrained by the limited availability of RNA profiling in routine settings. Here we introduce Mixture of Pathway Experts (MoPE), a knowledge-distillation framework that reframes multimodal learning as privileged distillation for histology-only inference. MoPE is motivated by the partial observability between RNA profiles and whole-slide images: histology can capture morphology-linked consequences of certain molecular programmes, but cannot be expected to reconstruct the full transcriptomic state. These results support pathway-structured privileged distillation as a promising route to using molecular information during training while preserving RNA-free inference.

When Evidence Vanishes: Distilling Visual-Evidence Dependency in Medical Vision-Language Models
Authors: Qizhen Lan, Yiling Ma, Ruiqi Chen, Mengchen Fan, Xi Xiao, Lin Zhao, Liang He, Baocheng Geng, Pulin Che, Tianyang Wang, Xiaoqian Jiang
First: 2026-10-16T13:48:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T13:48:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Compact medical vision-language models (VLMs) can answer clinical questions correctly without reliably using the image, relying instead on question wording or learned priors. Standard knowledge distillation copies a teacher's answer preference on the original image, but it leaves unchecked whether that preference is conditional on the visual evidence required by the question. We study this missing target as medical visual evidence dependency: support for the correct answer should decrease when the matched evidence is absent. We introduce Evidence-Conditioned Dependency Distillation (ECDD), which pairs each training image with a control matched to the evidence level and distills the teacher's drop in answer margin across the pair. A lower bound objective transfers positive dependency by penalizing only shortfalls, while original-image supervision preserves task accuracy. The same formulation applies when the evidence is a queried clinical region, a surgical instrument, or whole-image visual content. Across three medical VQA settings, ECDD keeps original-image accuracy competitive with distillation and alignment baselines while making answer support more sensitive to evidence removal. Under the DME headline objective, for example, the positive-case margin response to target rather than random masking rises from 0.786 with KD to 2.023 with ECDD, while original-image accuracy differs by 0.11 points. These results suggest that medical VLM distillation should transfer both the teacher's answer preference and its dependence on visual evidence.

Abstract
LLMs and LVLMs are increasingly deployed across heterogeneous hardware, ranging from data-center GPUs to resource-constrained edge devices. SETAREH Transform: A Sorting-Assisted Haar-Wavelet Sparsification Method for Approximate Inner-Product Computation
Authors: Seyed Kamaledin Setarehdan
First: 2026-10-16T11:42:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T11:42:29+00:00
Comments: 22 pages, 2 figures, 5 Tables, 21 References
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern deep learning systems increasingly rely on high-dimensional vector operations, in which large input and parameter vectors are repeatedly stored, transferred, and processed. Although substantial research has focused on reducing neural-network model size through pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and efficient architectures, the storage, memory-traffic, and communication costs associated with repeatedly presented high-dimensional input vectors remain important practical considerations, particularly in distributed and edge-computing environments. This paper proposes the SETAREH (Sorting-assisted Energy-concentrating Transform for Approximate Representation using Haar wavelets) transform, a Haar-wavelet-based sparsification approach for approximating the inner product between a fixed high-dimensional coefficient vector and successive input vectors. By applying the same sequence of permutations and Haar transforms to both vectors, SETAREH preserves the exact inner product before truncation. It then retains only a subset of the transformed coefficient-input pairs, reducing their representation cost while introducing a controllable approximation error. To evaluate SETAREH, we consider two complementary experimental settings: a synthetic Gaussian-vector baseline and a real apparent-age estimation task using a convolutional neural network on the APPA-REAL dataset. The results show that SETAREH can approximate the evaluated inner products using substantially fewer transformed coefficient-input pairs than the original vector dimension. The findings support its use as a potential structured data-representation method for repeated inner products with fixed weights, while the practical benefit depends on approximation accuracy and the cost of storing or transmitting permutation metadata.

Abstract
Speed-limit sign recognition on an automotive camera system-on-chip (SoC) must fit a detector and a classifier (the reader) into the memory, arithmetic, and energy budgets of the device. In our two-stage recognizer, the detector, a YOLO-family network with ReLU and hard-sigmoid operators, is compressed by channel replay: an iterative pruning cascade identifies the channels to remove, the recorded deletions are replayed on the original task-trained network, the parent, and one continuous knowledge-distillation recovery follows. On a YOLOv5n, replay removes 60.3% of the parameters at the accuracy of the parent and, in validation mAP50-95 over five matched seeds, outperforms iterative pruning and training from scratch, and its per-step variant (per-step replay) is the most accurate pruning method. On a YOLOv8n, replay and per-step replay outperform every other method and the unpruned parent. The reader, a five-layer CNN with 18k parameters, and the post-processing of the YOLOv5n and YOLOv8n heads use integer arithmetic only. With 4-bit weights and 8-bit activations (W4A8), the reader matches FP32 accuracy in 12.1 KB of on-chip memory, and an executable streaming model reproduces the INT8 class predictions of its runtime on all 9,850 test crops. On a Jetson AGX Orin, INT8 is faster than FP16 only in the implicit formulation of TensorRT, and the replay detector runs fastest on the deep-learning accelerator (DLA). Trained on public data only, the combined system of 0.72 M parameters recognizes 95.2% of the signs of a held-out automotive camera at 90% precision and 94 frames per second.

SPADE-KD: Peer-to-Peer Collaborative Learning under Heterogeneous Settings
Authors: Harikrishna Kuttivelil, Katia Obraczka

First: 2026-10-15T23:17:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T23:17:37+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Peer-to-peer collaborative learning is an emerging distributed deep learning paradigm that eliminates the need for a central aggregator and disseminator, avoiding the single-point-of-failure and bottleneck issues they can bring. Many such approaches use parameter averaging, requiring clients to have homogeneous model architectures, whereas others use knowledge distillation as an alternative while leaning on shared public data or centralized control infrastructure. Many of these approaches also make a one-size-fits-all assumption, i.e., jointly training a single, globally optimal solution for all clients, when in reality, clients' underlying data distributions might differ such that a globally optimal solution may not be locally optimal for all clients. To enable collaborative learning in environments with both model architecture and data distribution heterogeneity, we present SPADE-KD -- a fully decentralized model distillation framework that optimizes collaboration towards personalized rather than global performance. SPADE-KD leverages two mechanisms -- adaptive distillation and utility-driven peer selection. Adaptive distillation modulates the strength of each incoming model transfer by the performance advantage of the incoming model compared to the native model measured by their task losses, such that a client will distill more aggressively from incoming models that outperform it on its own local data. Utility-driven peer selection uses the loss-informed utility derived from adaptive distillation to rank prospective partners, focusing on exchanges that measurably improve the local model while suppressing low-utility transfers. Using federated classification tasks, we demonstrate that SPADE-KD consistent outperforms naive decentralized distillation with lower loss and faster convergence and that its individual components are more effective together than independently.

Abstract
Traditional backdoor attacks inject artificial trigger patterns into training data, causing models to misclassify triggered inputs while maintaining normal behavior on clean samples. Since these artificial triggers are absent in clean datasets, standard knowledge distillation (KD) typically eliminates them, leading to the common belief that KD serves as an effective purification defense. In this paper, we propose DAR (Discover-and-Relabel), a support-persistent backdoor method that retains its behavior even after clean-data distillation. DAR identifies naturally abundant patterns in low-dimensional feature subspaces using subspace clustering and applies label-only poisoning to samples that inherently satisfy the discovered rule. At test time, the backdoor is activated by locally modifying the corresponding subspace of the target images. By instantiating spatial and frequency operators, DAR achieves attack accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art backdoor attacks in ImageNet classification and CLIP-based prompt tuning. Notably, DAR keeps attack success above 96% after clean KD and remains defense-resistant in practice. Project page: https://junhoo.me/backdoor

Abstract
Latent chain-of-thought replaces written reasoning steps with continuous hidden states, giving models additional steps to compute before answering. It remains unclear what computation these steps actually perform. High task accuracy and decodable intermediate answers do not establish that a model uses its latent chain to reach an answer.
