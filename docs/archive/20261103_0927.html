Abstract
Vector Quantization (VQ) is a rapidly evolving technique for compressing Large Language Model (LLM) weights, reducing memory usage, and speeding up inference. However, extreme compression deteriorates model performance, which is challenging to recover due to the discrete nature of VQ parameters. Existing methods employ training-free approaches that are limited to layer-wise reconstruction loss minimization. To address this limitation, we propose TVQ, a novel method that leverages straight-through estimation to enable gradient descent-based optimization of vector-quantized LLMs. By reformulating VQ LLM optimization with trainable latent weights, TVQ enables arbitrary objective minimization. We constitute our approach by applying TVQ in a block-wise knowledge distillation paradigm, which balances quality and training efficiency, and propose several practical solutions to accelerate training. In experiments on modern LLMs, we demonstrate that TVQ is compatible with modern VQ schemes. As a result, TVQ achieves state-of-the-art quality in low-bit post-training quantization settings with a modest training budget and straightforward PyTorch implementation. For instance, TVQ achieves near-lossless 2-bit quantization for LLaMA2-70B with only 1% accuracy drop on popular Question-Answering benchmarks.

MuSID-KD: Multigranularity Knowledge Distillation for Generative Recommendation with Semantic IDs
Authors: Fengshi Zhang, Xiyan Ding, Shuxiao Xie, Wei Zhang
First: 2026-10-26T11:09:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T14:28:33+00:00
Comments: 12 pages, including appendix
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generative recommendation with semantic IDs (SIDs) represents each item as a sequence of discrete tokens and autoregressively predicts the next item's SID. Despite strong recommendation performance, repeated decoding over multiple SID positions incurs substantial inference cost. Knowledge distillation transfers knowledge from a powerful generative teacher to a lightweight student, yet conventional objectives do not fully account for multi-token SID generation. We identify two limitations: item-level distribution alignment may not adequately preserve the teacher's ranking of candidate groups sharing SID prefixes, while uniform alignment across SID positions may affect final item ranking differently. We propose MuSID-KD, which combines Hierarchical Candidate Distribution Alignment (HCDA) and Ranking Impact Guided Levelwise Distillation (RIGD). HCDA aggregates item probabilities according to shared SID prefixes and aligns candidate distributions at successive levels, from candidate groups to individual items. RIGD evaluates the reduction in pairwise ranking surrogate loss when one student score contribution is replaced with the teacher's contribution, and allocates supervision accordingly. Experiments on real-world recommendation datasets using three lightweight student architectures demonstrate improved recommendation accuracy and better alignment of prefix marginal distributions and pairwise item rankings, without modifying the student's online inference architecture. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/m7k2-review-83f9/.

Abstract
Per-token gating of forward/reverse KL losses has become a standard technique for on-policy knowledge distillation (OPD), but existing methods such as EOPD (Jin et al., 2026) and ToDi (Jung et al., 2025) each fix a single gating signal and a single gating direction, and the two have never been compared directly. We introduce a four-coefficient parameterization lambda_t = sigma(a * h_t + b * u(x) + c + d * gap_t) in which direction-aligned proxies of EOPD and ToDi appear as one-dimensional (1D) restrictions, and which adds multi-channel composition and an explicit bias as further degrees of freedom. On TweetEval (Barbieri et al., 2020) emotion and hate, with a Qwen3-32B teacher and a Qwen3-4B student, configurations in the full family reach higher accuracy than the matched-magnitude single-channel (entropy-only / gap-only) 1D restrictions in 33 of 36 comparable cells, and a 26-cell mean-match isolation experiment places dynamic gating ahead of effective-KL-matched static baselines in 19 of 26 cells. Because cells share training data, models, and parameter substructure, we report both counts as exploratory aggregate directional evidence rather than as independent hypothesis tests. Targeted three-seed paired replications of the nine headline comparisons singled out by that sweep -- including a third task, offensive -- are directionally consistent, but individually smaller than the single-seed estimates and not significant at n=3. We therefore present the parameterization primarily as a shared coordinate system for comparing per-token gating designs in short-output classification OPD.

Distilling Knowledge from Search: Decoder Model Factory for Quantum LDPC Codes
Authors: Anil Bilgin, Wanda Hou, Pranav Srinivas, Yi-Zhuang You
First: 2026-10-30T08:09:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T08:09:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce GraphMP, a learning-based message-passing decoder for quantum low-density parity-check codes that transfers without fine-tuning across code families, code sizes, physical error rates, and noise models. GraphMP uses local message passing to keep computation scalable as the decoding graph grows. Guided by its predictions, beam search progressively refines candidate error patterns, trading additional decoding time for lower logical error rates. We develop the decoder model factory as a paradigm for training successive models on experience generated by earlier models during decoding. Model-guided search supplies verified trajectories for knowledge distillation, folding computation spent on decoding into reusable training data. Repeated collection and distillation improve the decoder across generations and adapt its strategy as the noise changes. These results motivate a coupled quantum--classical architecture with two interacting cycles: rapid decoding protects quantum information, while slower learning renews the models from operating experience. The long-term vision is a scalable decoder model factory that produces high-performance, low-latency decoders and continually adapts them to evolving quantum hardware.

Abstract
Pre-trained models offer complementary strengths in CT organ segmentation, but their relative accuracy varies across organs and spatial regions. Multi-teacher knowledge distillation can combine these strengths in a single student, yet global or instance-level teacher weights overlook variation within a 3D volume. We propose Task-Performance Routing (TPR), which uses region-wise segmentation errors to determine teacher weights during training without learned routing parameters. In hard regions, TPR favors teachers with lower prediction errors; in easy regions, it uses uniform weights to combine their knowledge. A hierarchical spatial partition provides fine-grained routing in shallow encoder stages and coarse-grained routing in deep stages. Experiments on BTCV, MSD-Liver, and MSD-Pancreas with two teacher pairs and two backbone architectures show higher Dice scores than the individual teachers, adaptive ensembles, and evaluated multi-teacher distillation baselines. On MSD-Pancreas, TPR achieves Dice scores of 65.40% with SwinUNETR and 68.25% with U-Net, compared with 64.38% and 67.45% for the corresponding adaptive ensembles. Inference uses the student alone, with no routing or teacher overhead. Code is available at https://github.com/EdvinCecilia/TPR_MultiTeacher.

Multiple Instance Learning is the predominant method for Whole Slide Image classification in digital pathology, enabling the use of slide-level labels to supervise model training. Although MIL eliminates the tedious fine-grained annotation process for supervised learning, whether it can learn accurate bag- and instance-level classifiers remains a question. Abstract
Accurate soil moisture estimation is critical for precision agriculture, influencing irrigation, fertilization, and harvesting strategies. Traditional methods primarily rely on meteorological and soil property data, while recent approaches incorporate visual modalities such as aerial and geospatial imagery. Abstract
In segmentation-based continuous sign language recognition, an untrimmed video is first partitioned into lexical units; segmentation is therefore a means to an end---downstream recognition. However, segmenters are usually judged by local boundary detection rather than by the quality of the lexical segments and the recognition they enable. We present BEACON, a begin/end-aware segmenter that combines four-class Outside/Begin/Inside/End (BIOE) labeling, constrained Viterbi decoding over eight legal transitions, and boundary knowledge distilled from a binary teacher into a shared student. Under strict no-cut evaluation with validation-based model selection, BEACON improves segment-F1 by 14.58/14.59 points and boundary-F1 by 2.17/1.22 points, and reduces the downstream word error rate (WER) by 9.10/4.15 points relative to the state-of-the-art in the PHOENIX-14/BSL Corpus. To examine which segmentation metric better reflects downstream recognition, we evaluated five distinct architecture families. Segment-F1 aligns more consistently with lower WER than boundary-F1 in both corpora (Spearman $ρ=-1.0/-0.9$ versus $-0.9/-0.8$). These results support evaluating sign language segmentation in terms of segment quality and downstream recognition utility rather than local boundary accuracy alone.

Abstract
Accurately predicting the transport properties of liquid electrolytes is critical for next-generation battery design and requires force fields that are both quantum-mechanically accurate and fast enough for long-timescale simulation. Recent large-scale pretrained machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) approach density-functional-theory accuracy, but remain too slow to access the necessary timescales to converge transport properties. Abstract
Model organisms (MOs) - language models trained to exhibit undesired or unnatural behaviours - are frequently used as testbeds for evaluating white-box interpretability techniques. Current MOs are typically constructed via post-hoc supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Prior research has shown that interpretability methods can easily identify hidden behaviours in these MOs. However, recent work suggests that such post-hoc training methods may make interpretability unrealistically easy. We investigate this claim by constructing a suite of 61 $\verb|OLMo2-1B|$- and $\verb|gemma-3-1b-it|$-based MOs trained with seven different techniques, including standard post-hoc SFT, post-hoc DPO, and more realistic integration of MO data into the OLMo post-training DPO phase. We also use these to train 96 further variants via knowledge distillation. We use these MO variants to benchmark activation oracles, activation steering, the logit lens, and the Jacobian lens. Our findings show that (i) MO interpretability depends strongly on training objective, target behaviour, model architecture, and training data generation pipeline; (ii) substantial variance remains even after controlling for differences in the strength of target behaviour expression; and (iii) both our more realistic $\textit{integrated training}$ and knowledge distillation often yield less interpretable MOs than standard post-hoc methods. Our results cast substantial doubt on the validity of current MOs as interpretability proxies and suggest actionable steps towards more robust benchmarks.

Abstract
Most deep learning systems for diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading are evaluated on a single data source, with limited evidence of cross-site generalisation, uncertainty calibration, and interpretability. We propose CalibCause, a quality-gated mixture-of-experts framework that combines per-expert visual explanations, label-free image-quality conditioning, knowledge distillation, and calibrated confidence with deferral. We evaluated the framework using 51,310 fundus images from EyePACS, DDR, and APTOS 2019, with IDRiD and DeepDRiD for external testing. Evaluation included pooled testing, leave-one-environment-out experiments, paired bootstrap confidence intervals, and local grade-threshold adaptation. On the pooled test split, the label-free distilled student achieved quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) of 0.863 and accuracy of 0.865 with 27.8 million parameters, 52% fewer than its teacher. Across held-out environments, the label-free model achieved mean QWK of 0.741 compared with 0.743 for a plain ConvNeXt-Tiny baseline, while adversarial invariance did not improve cross-site grading accuracy. Refitting grade thresholds using 200 labelled images increased held-out EyePACS QWK from 0.533 to 0.621 for the label-free model. After vector scaling, expected calibration error ranged from 0.025 to 0.036 across CalibCause variants, and deferring the least confident 20% of images increased student accuracy from 0.865 to 0.923 on the remaining images. These findings demonstrate that quality-aware grading, interpretable expert routing, model compression, and calibrated deferral can be integrated without sacrificing cross-site performance relative to a plain baseline, although reliable deployment still requires local validation and adaptation.

Information-Theoretic Reliability--Safety Bounds and Finite-Alignment Dynamics in AI Knowledge Distillation

Abstract
The reliability and safety of artificial intelligence (AI), together with their transfer and evolution through knowledge distillation, have attracted growing attention. We establish an information-theoretic bound on the reliability--safety trade-off and characterize the dynamics of this trade-off under knowledge distillation. We first prove a universal trade-off inequality that is independent of the specific model form. Its boundary is determined solely by a single parameter Kh, which quantifies the hidden layer's ability to distinguish benign from hazardous inputs. We then develop a canonical Boltzmann-machine model of AI response behavior and derive two exact relations between reliability and safety. The trade-off curve is determined by the hazard discrimination capacity K, while the overall answer tendency controls the operating point along the curve. These relations agree well with existing experimental data. In knowledge distillation, we find that teacher responses induce a renormalization of the student model's trade-off parameters. We further use a renormalization-group approach to study multigenerational distillation, deriving exact recursion relations and the associated renormalization-group-like flows for two representative direct intergenerational distillation protocols at arbitrary teacher--student alignment strength. These flows reveal fixed-point structures, long-term dynamics, and critical behavior, yielding testable predictions for the critical transition between capability loss and stable transmission.

You Don't Need Aligned Representations: Knowledge Distillation via Random Prototype Spaces
Authors: Hamza Etcibasi, Ramazan Gokberk Cinbis
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-27T20:27:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T20:27:47+00:00
Comments: To appear at NeurIPS 2026. 10 pages main text plus appendices. Code: https://github.com/HamzaEtcibasi/RPKD
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
A widely held assumption in knowledge distillation is that teacher and student representations must occupy a compatible or explicitly aligned feature space. We propose RPKD (Random Prototype Knowledge Distillation), which discards this assumption by comparing both networks against a shared bank of randomly initialized, frozen prototype vectors. Alignment is therefore carried out in an external reference space rather than in the teacher's native feature coordinates: the teacher side is projected by fixed random maps that carry no trainable parameters, and only the student adapts. Two branches handle the transfer: a logit branch aligning global prototype similarity distributions and a feature branch matching spatially-resolved prototype activation maps. Both operate within the same fixed prototype space, which can be interpreted as a random feature embedding that approximately preserves inter-sample similarity structure, so the prototype bank and the distillation objective stay unchanged across teacher-student pairs while only the input projections are instantiated per architecture. In low-category settings, decoupling the prototype vocabulary from the task label space yields richer supervision than the label space alone, an advantage absent from logit-based methods, whose supervisory signal collapses as class count shrinks. This advantage is especially relevant in real-world scenarios where the label space is typically constrained. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of RPKD, achieving a maximum gain of +7.98% over OFA on CIFAR-100, +12.44% on ImageNet-100, and +1.28% on ImageNet-1K. Source code is available at https://github.com/HamzaEtcibasi/RPKD

Depth through Recurrence: Towards Ultra-Efficient On-Device ASR

Abstract
Modern automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems have achieved remarkable accuracy by scaling model depth and capacity, but at the cost of increased memory, energy, and computational demands. On edge devices where ASR is often most needed, such as watches and glasses, deploying such large models is infeasible due to extremely constrained resources. This raises a fundamental question: can we achieve high representational power in deep ASR models without scaling up parameterization? In this work, we revisit the role of depth and identify layer wise representational dynamics, in which most layers learn functionally similar transformations. Motivated by this insight, we propose a block-recurrent ASR architecture that replaces parameterized depth with a small set of recurrent blocks, each consisting of weight-shared layers, thereby preserving effective depth while drastically reducing model size. Through representation-guided grouping and knowledge distillation, block-recurrent models retain the accuracy of large reference models while using an order of magnitude fewer parameters. Across Open ASR Leaderboard benchmarks, a model with only two shared blocks recovers 97.4% of the accuracy of large models on average. The compact backbone further enables efficient on-device personalization through lightweight MoE-LoRA adaptation, allowing user-specific models to match or surpass the accuracy of general-purpose ASR systems with significantly lower inference cost. By reusing weights across recurrent steps, the proposed architecture reduces memory-access overhead and inference energy consumption. Together, these results show that high quality ASR can be achieved without large parameterization, and that structured recurrence provides a principled path towards ultra-compressed, energy-efficient, and personalized speech recognition on the edge.

Abstract

Abstract
Spike activity has been the dominant neural signal for behavior decoding because its high spatiotemporal resolution supports accurate decoding. However, as intracortical brain-computer interfaces (iBCIs) move toward higher channel counts and wireless operation, the high sampling rates required to record spikes create substantial power and bandwidth demands. Local field potentials (LFPs) offer complementary advantages, including greater long-term stability, lower energy consumption, and lower bandwidth requirements. However, LFP-based decoders often achieve lower accuracy and rely on non-causal architectures that cannot be used directly for real-time deployment. We propose REALM, a retrospective knowledge distillation (RKD) framework for causal LFP behavior decoding. Inspired by offline-to-online distillation in speech recognition, REALM transfers non-causal representational knowledge from a pretrained, multi-session bidirectional LFP teacher to a causal student model. We first pretrain a bidirectional Mamba-2 teacher across multiple recording sessions using continuous masked autoencoding (CMAE), and then distill its representation into a compact causal student using a combined objective of representation alignment and autoencoding. Abstract
